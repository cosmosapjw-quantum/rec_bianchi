`sum_j N_j=N`, `sum_j epsilon_j N_j=E`, `N_j>=0`.

`min_j epsilon_j <= E/N <= max_j epsilon_j`.

Necessity follows because `E/N` is a convex combination; sufficiency follows by assigning mass to the two energies bracketing the mean. #### Frame, measure and sign convention

Let `n^a` be the normal observer, `u_H^a=gamma(n^a+beta^i e_i^a)` the hydrogen observer, and a future-directed photon be

`p^a = E_N (n^a + ell_N^a)`, with `ell_N^a n_a=0`, `ell_N^a ell^N_a=1` and `E_N=-p.n>0`.

Define, rather than assume, the Doppler factor

`D_(H<-N) = (-p.u_H)/(-p.n) = gamma(1-beta.ell_N)`,

so `nu_H=D_(H<-N) nu_N`; obtain `ell_H` by projecting this same `p^a` into the hydrogen tetrad. The distribution `f(x,p)` is a scalar. The mass-shell measure `dPi=d^3p/p^0` is invariant, while discrete frequency/solid-angle weights are frame dependent and must be generated from this same map. Equivalently, `I_nu/nu^3` is invariant; copying an ordinate while changing its frame label is not.

The physical moments used as independent frame oracles are

`N^a = integral p^a f dPi`,

`T^(ab) = integral p^a p^b f dPi`.

They must transform as a vector and tensor under the tetrad boost. This is stronger than comparing two arrays at nominally equal `(nu,direction)` indices.

Two conventions remain blocking inputs to that construction. Second, a rate quoted in the hydrogen rest frame is not automatically an occupation derivative per normal-observer coordinate time.
